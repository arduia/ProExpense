The Pocket Coin is locked. This doc only asks: what does the coin look like up close?

Five axes of refinement, four to six variations each. Nothing here changes the silhouette, the palette, or the "pe" idea. We're just deciding what kind of coin it is — milled or smooth, struck or stamped, alone or paired.

01 / The anchor

What we're refining from.

Round 02's committed mark, on its two primary surfaces. Everything that follows in this doc keeps the four constants on the right and only changes one variable at a time.

02 / Axis A · Edge

Six ways to finish the rim.

The edge is the most-read 20% of the mark. The current dashed inner ring reads as "perforated" — fine, but generic. Below: six rim languages, each with a different physical reference. Pick the one whose noun matches the brand: milled coin, beaded medallion, double-line stamp, gear-tooth token, broken-strike artifact, smooth bullion.

Axis A · Edge

The rim language.

A·01
Reeded edge
Tick-marks around the rim like a milled quarter. Reads as currency immediately.
A·02
Beaded rim
Tiny dots inside the outer ring — a medallion / commemorative-coin tell. Premium without trying.
A·03
Double thin ring
Two equal hairlines. Reads as stamp or seal. Cleaner at 16px than dashes.
A·04
Gear / token notch
Subway-token energy. May read fintech-y / machine. Save for a "tools" sub-brand.
A·05
Broken strike
Outer ring with intentional gaps — looks worn, hand-pressed. Has voice. Hard to make consistent.
A·06
Smooth bullion
A single thicker ring, no inner detail. Most modern, scales best to 16px. Risks looking plain.
A·07
Micro-dot inner
Dashed → micro-dotted. Same idea as R02 but finer. Most evolutionary path.
A·08
Stitched rim
Larger dashes — reads as wallet stitching. Ties to "pocket" metaphor.
03 / Axis B · Glyph

The "pe" itself, six ways.

The italic serif "pe" is the signature inside the coin. It can sit upright or slope, ligature or stand apart, take a flourish or not. None of these change the silhouette — only the letterform inside it.

Axis B · Glyph

The letter inside.

B·01
R02 baseline
Instrument Serif italic at 62pt. The reference point.
B·02
Custom pe ligature
Shared midline between p-bowl and e-eye. Ownable, distinct from default Instrument Serif.
B·03
Roman upright
No italic. Sturdier, more "official mint" feel. Loses the signature warmth.
B·04
Geist Bold
Sans, heavy, geometric. Modern but breaks the editorial tone. Off-system.
B·05
Small caps PE
Inscription-style. Reads as memorial coin. Loses the casual notebook tone.
B·06
Big p, super-small e
Asymmetric, almost a chemical symbol. Distinctive but reads "Pₑ".
B·07
p · e (with bullet)
Separated by a center-dot — reads as initials on a calling card. Quieter.
B·08
Double-strike
Half-pixel offset second layer — letterpress ghost. Adds tactile feel; vanishes at small sizes.
04 / Axis C · Inscription

What's written on the rim.

Real coins carry inscriptions: dates, mottoes, mint marks. We can borrow that vocabulary very lightly — not on the app icon (too small) but on marketing, splash, and large-format applications. Below: five rim-language ideas.

Axis C · Inscription

Rim text & micro-marks.

C·01
Full rim inscription
Coin-style: name on top arc, year on bottom. For splash + brand book only — illegible at app-icon size.
C·02
Four compass dots
N · E · S · W. Reads as traveller's coin. Ties to multi-currency / Maya, Siti, Carlos, Aiko story.
C·03
Currency cardinal points
€ ¥ £ ฿ at compass positions. Most literal "multi-currency" expression. Charming, slightly busy.
C·04
North-star marker
One mint-mark at 12 o'clock. Quiet, classic. Almost invisible at small size — that's fine.
C·05
Year stamp below
Mint-mark year under the glyph. Use for anniversary editions / yearly recaps in-app.
C·06
Tally-mark foot
Five tally lines = the notebook metaphor in geometry. Ties to "log it" verb. Conceptually strongest.

06 / Axis E · Physicality

Flat, struck, or stamped.

Same vector mark, three rendering registers. Flat is what ships in the app today. Struck (subtle inner shadow) is what App Store / marketing benefits from. Stamped (offset / embossed) is the most "object-like" but most expensive to maintain. Pick the floor and the ceiling.

Axis E · Physicality

How real is the coin.

E·01
Flat — minimum spec
Strokes + type only. Works at 16px. The floor.
E·02
Struck — gentle relief
Soft radial light, 0.6px shadow under outer ring, 1.2px ghost on glyph. Use for splash, Play Store, hero.
E·03
Embossed on paper
Tone-on-tone, pressed-in feel. For premium swag, business cards, no-color contexts. Useless on screen.

07 / Shortlist

Three combinations worth striking.

Mixing the axes. These are the three I'd hand to a stamping die. Each is named, has a stated job, and is a single decision the team can argue about — not a forest of variations.

Three recommendations.

A·02 · A·07 · A·08 ⨯ B·02 ⨯ C·02 ⨯ E·02
Pick 01 · safest evolution

The Beaded Coin

R02 baseline + A·02 beaded inner ring. One change, immediately upgrades the medallion read. Drops cleanly to 16px (beads disappear gracefully). Ship this if we ship anything.

Pick 02 · narrative

The Traveller's Coin

Smooth outer + four compass dots (C·02) + R02 glyph. Telegraphs multi-currency and pocket without literal $/€/¥. Plays well with the four-persona story (Maya · Siti · Carlos · Aiko).

Pick 03 · bold

The Stitched Pocket

Stitched rim (A·08) + struck physicality (E·02) + R02 glyph. Doubles down on the "pocket / wallet" half of the metaphor. Most distinctive in a Play Store grid. Higher production cost — needs PNG at sizes where SVG can't.
